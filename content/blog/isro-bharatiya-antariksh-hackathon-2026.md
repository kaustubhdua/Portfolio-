In 2026 I took part in the **ISRO Bharatiya Antariksh Hackathon**, a national innovation initiative presented by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and powered by Hack2skill. I received a certificate of participation for a successful idea submission.

## The problem

We tackled **satellite-assisted environmental monitoring**. The prototype ingests live datasets from India's Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) together with NASA FIRMS satellite feeds, in order to detect air-quality index trends and HCHO (formaldehyde) pollution hotspots in real time.

## Why two kinds of data

Ground stations and satellites see different things.

- **CPCB monitoring stations** report measured air quality at specific places. They are direct readings, but they only exist where a station has been installed.
- **NASA FIRMS** (the Fire Information for Resource Management System) delivers near-real-time satellite detections of active fires and thermal anomalies. A satellite sees wide areas at once, but its view is indirect and needs to be matched to what is happening on the ground.

Putting the two side by side is what makes a hotspot worth flagging: a pattern in the readings that a satellite signal can help explain, or a satellite signal that the ground data can confirm.

## What I practised

The challenge pulled together three skills I enjoy using on real problems: **full-stack development** to make the result usable, **spatial algorithms** to reason about where things are, and **data processing** to turn live feeds into something a person can act on.

## An honest label

This was a hackathon prototype, and the certificate is one of participation. I mention that because I like projects to say plainly what they are. I am grateful for the chance to point those skills at a real space-technology challenge.
